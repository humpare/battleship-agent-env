Battleship: Break the Agent

A 5x5 Battleship environment (Harbor + MCP) where an exact, coded opponent punishes every imprecise move. Entry for AfterQuery's challenge at Junction 2026.

The task

  • Two-player Battleship on a 5x5 board, played through four MCP tools: get_rules, observe, place_fleet, fire.
  • Five ships each (3, 2, 2, 1, 1), straight, never touching. A hit shoots again; a miss hands over the turn.
  • The opponent's method is disclosed: it lists every fleet still possible and shoots the most likely cell.
  • Score between 0 and 1, recomputed by a separate verifier that replays the game server's private log.

Where ships can be: share of the 5,772 legal fleets with a ship in each cell

Corners 60.8%, centre 52.3%, the four cells diagonally next to a corner 3.1%. The opponent shoots the likely cells first, so a good fleet leaves the corners empty.

Why this is a fair test, not a trick

  • Nothing is withheld. The rules tell the agent how the opponent places its fleet and how it chooses every shot.
  • Both decisions follow from the rules. Where to shoot: the grid above. Where to place: the opponent shoots likely cells first, so a fleet with empty corners makes it miss.
  • Placement alone moves the score from 0.24 to 0.99. That is the expected score of an exact shooter with the worst and the best of the 5,772 fleets; the average fleet gives 0.59.
  • Simpler coded players land in between (30 boards): exact shooting with a random fleet 0.663; checkerboard hunt 0.356; random shots 0.115.
  • The game is short: about 15 tool calls, so context length and tool errors play no part.

Why the agent fails

  • Placement: in 15 of 15 games it put a ship on a corner, the cells the opponent shoots first. It ended with 1.3 of 9 ship cells unhit on average; the reference ended with 8.8.
  • Shooting: 44% of its shots were not at a best cell, and it opened on the centre in 14 of 15 games.
  • Copying: in 11 of 15 games it placed exactly the example fleet printed in the rules. That fleet is worth 0.44; the best is worth 0.99.

Each of these is visible move by move in the replays, for example a shot at a 15% cell while an 85% cell was open.

Reward integrity

  • The score is recomputed, not reported. The game server keeps a private action log; a separate verifier container without network replays it and writes one number between 0 and 1 to /logs/verifier/reward.txt.
  • Partial credit: a loss scores half the share of enemy ship cells hit; a win scores 0.5 plus half the share of the agent's own cells still unhit.
  • Doing nothing scores 0, and so does placing a fleet and stopping.
  • Stopping early never pays: the score never falls as play continues, and any win (at least 0.556) beats any unfinished game (at most 0.444).
  • Four cheating scripts through Harbor all scored 0: forged reward files and a forged log, junk and illegal requests, place-and-stop, and sixty simultaneous shots at one cell (one counted).
  • 68 automated tests cover replay, illegal and repeated requests, leaks of hidden cells, and that every opponent shot can be rebuilt from public information.

Qwen2.5-3B with a LoRA adapter, trained to imitate the exact player and tested on boards it never saw. A second model trained on seven rule sets kept its shot quality on rules it had never seen (0.93 against 0.45 untrained), including a 6x6 board, but could not place a legal fleet for unseen fleet compositions.

The trade-off: it is a specialist. We did not measure its other abilities, and narrow training of this kind usually costs general ability.
